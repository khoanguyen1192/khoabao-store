Read the marks. Not a made-up price.

Assay decodes British silver hallmarks — London, Birmingham and Sheffield, 1773 to 1975 — by looking the punches up in the published date-letter tables. It runs entirely on your device, and when the marks cannot settle a year it says so instead of inventing one.

Assay never states what a piece is worth, never judges whether it is genuine, and never uses the word “appraisal”. Those are the three things the marks alone cannot tell you.

What it does

Decodes hallmarks. Tell it which town mark, which date letter, how the letter is cut and what shape the punch is, and it narrows 609 recorded date letters down to the cycles that actually match. About half the time that is a single year. The rest of the time it names the candidates and stops.

Dates stamped numbers. US patent numbers, UK registered-design (Rd) numbers, the UK diamond registry mark and ceramic backstamps, all from published tables.

Tells you what not to do to it. The single most expensive mistake with inherited silver is cleaning it before anyone has seen it. Assay says so before you touch it.

Shows what each selling route would really net. Start from what a dealer would ask in a shop; Assay applies each route’s real fee schedule and shows the spread and the time it takes. Estimates from published fee schedules, not financial advice.

Privacy

No account, no upload, no analytics. Your photographs stay on your device and the app works with no connection at all. See the privacy policy.
